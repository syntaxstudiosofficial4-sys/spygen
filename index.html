The problem with the feed

Most networks are built to be endless. Spygen is built to end.

Infinite scroll, public counters and algorithmic ranking reward volume over meaning. Spygen is designed around water instead of a pipe — content arrives on a tide, reactions ripple privately, and relationships form only through real back-and-forth.

What most networks do

  • An algorithmic feed that never runs out
  • Public like and follower counts as status
  • Following someone with a single tap
  • Ranking content by engagement velocity

What Spygen does instead

  • A tide of content that refreshes twice a day, then stops
  • Private echoes that surface as felt depth, not numbers
  • Moorings that form only after real exchange
  • Drift physics: time, care and closeness, not virality

Calls

Small capsules of text or voice you cast into the water, aimed at the open Sound or a single Cove.

Echoes

A private resonance you send back. It never shows as a count — only as how deep and far the call has drifted.

Coves

Inlets formed around a shared interest. You join by casting something in, not by subscribing.

Moorings

A steady connection that forms after enough back-and-forth on a Tide Line — never by a single follow tap.

Built for every tide

Accessibility and responsiveness

  • Every tab, button and toggle is reachable and operable by keyboard, with a visible focus ring.
  • Reduced-motion preferences stop the 3D scene, the aurora background and every card animation.
  • Text keeps AA contrast against ink and panel backgrounds throughout.
  • Live regions announce echoes and new calls for screen reader users.
  • Layout reflows from a single column on small phones up to the full four-panel demo on desktop.
  • No content relies on color alone — icons and text carry meaning together.
